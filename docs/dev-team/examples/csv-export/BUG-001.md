## Steps to Reproduce

1. Go to `/reports` as a finance analyst
2. Apply a filter that includes order **#10481** (customer `Doe, Jane`)
3. Click **Export**
4. Open the downloaded CSV in Google Sheets (or parse it with `csv-parse`)

---

## Expected Result

`Doe, Jane` occupies a single **Customer** cell, and all columns after it stay aligned — per `requirements.md` must-have #3 (Safe field encoding) and test case TC-010.

---

## Actual Result

The customer value is written **unquoted** as `Doe, Jane`, so `Jane` spills into the next column and every subsequent column on that row shifts one to the right. The header row and any rows without commas look correct, so the corruption is easy to miss on a quick glance.

---

## Evidence

- Raw line in the file: `10481,Doe, Jane,shipped,2026-05-19,...`
  Expected: `10481,"Doe, Jane",shipped,2026-05-19,...`
- Screenshot: Google Sheets import showing the `status`/`date` columns shifted right for order #10481.
- `csv-parse` reports that row #10481 has one more field than the header.

---

## Hypothesis

The row serializer most likely joins values with `cols.join(',')` and never applies RFC-4180 quoting — values are not wrapped in double-quotes when they contain the delimiter, a quote, or a newline. Switching to a compliant CSV writer (wrap fields containing `,` `"` or newline in `"`, and double any internal `"`) should fix this and also resolve the embedded-newline half of TC-010.

---

## Workaround

None. Users cannot reliably re-quote the data after the fact, and the misalignment is silent.
